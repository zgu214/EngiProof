# EngiProof Publication Strategy

## Purpose

Publication is a strategic layer of EngiProof, not the complete moat.

> **The journal paper is one layer of the moat, not the moat itself.**

The deeper moat is the accumulated, verified engineering evidence system, the engineering judgment encoded in its rules, and the ability to extend that system rigorously over time.

## Paper A

**Working title**

> EngiProof: A Provenance-Preserving Framework for Converting Published Engineering Research into Reproducible Computational Evidence

**Primary target journal**

Advances in Engineering Software.

**Purpose**

Paper A should establish:
- the engineering-evidence lifecycle;
- provenance and source-identity rules;
- the distinction between reproduction and independent verification;
- discrepancy preservation/classification;
- human review and promotion control;
- machine-auditable evidence graphs;
- explicit engineering-qualification boundaries.

It should not be presented primarily as:
- a PDF parser;
- an LLM application;
- a paper-to-Python generator;
- a collection of subsea case studies.

The case studies are validation evidence for the methodology.

## Evidence-count strategy

Do not optimize for maximum paper count.

A strong first methodology paper needs enough heterogeneous cases to demonstrate generality and failure-aware behavior.

Current planning basis:
- P40–P44 already provide five heterogeneous evidence cases;
- P45 should add nonlinear dynamic/time-dependent mechanics;
- after P45, reassess submission readiness;
- P46 is optional and must fill a defined evidence gap.

A working target of **6–8 strong heterogeneous studies** is sufficient for Paper A if the cases are deep and traceable.

## Publication sequence

1. Paper A — EngiProof methodology / evidence lifecycle.
2. Software publication later if useful — implementation, APIs, reproducibility and software reuse.
3. Domain-specific papers may follow from deep evidence cases.
4. Standards/recommended-practice activity comes only after independent external use and replication.

## Strategic principle

Publication provides:
- priority;
- peer review;
- citation;
- external scrutiny;
- academic legitimacy;
- terminology that others can reuse.

Publication does not by itself provide:
- industrial trust;
- engineering qualification;
- legal exclusivity;
- community;
- standards adoption;
- a verified evidence corpus.

Those arise from continued engineering evidence, software quality and external use.

## Long-term objective

Move progressively from:

```text
Tool
  ↓
Product
  ↓
Category
  ↓
Methodology
  ↓
Academic recognition
  ↓
Engineering adoption
  ↓
Recommended practice
  ↓
Influence on standards
```

The journal article should help establish the methodology early enough that EngiProof can continue growing far beyond the publication itself.
