\begin{abstract}
Published engineering results are difficult to reuse computationally without losing provenance. When a paper is internally inconsistent or omits an input, a reader or a code port tends to normalise the problem away by choosing one of two printed values, inferring a missing unit, or tuning an unknown until the printed number is matched. This paper presents \engiproof, a framework that converts selected published engineering methods into source-bounded, reproducible and independently checked evidence. It keeps six things explicitly separated and machine-auditable: what was published, what was reproduced, what was checked independently, what disagrees, what was decided by a human, and what remains unqualified.

The evidence model distinguishes \eclass{PUBLISHED}, \eclass{INDEPENDENT} and the reserved \eclass{SOLVER\_NEW} evidence, and records each target as reproduced, compared, conditional or blocked. Discrepancies are classified in a controlled taxonomy, and the promotion gate they impose can be cleared only by an append-only human decision. Each study carries an evidence graph. Verification recomputes in isolation and never mutates the evidence it verifies, and engineering qualification remains outside the system (\estatus{NOT\_GRANTED} in every case).

The framework is evaluated by capability, not by paper count, on six heterogeneous offshore and subsea structural-mechanics studies published between 1976 and 2019. The studies span four publishers and societies and include a scanned 1983 source, and their mechanics range from closed-form collapse to nonlinear time integration. Source-bounded implementations reproduce 14 targets to source precision and compare 19 more; independent formulations decide the direction of five numerical conflicts; and 14 discrepancy records are preserved without tuning. Where a source lacks the inputs for a target, the outcome is recorded as blocked and the missing parameters are named. The framework's own failures are reported as evidence as well, among them a verification step that rewrote 20 tracked result files and recomputation noise that surfaced only on other operating systems; each was found by the evidence process, recorded, and corrected or bounded. The contribution is a methodology and its evidence; the paper makes no claim of autonomous discovery or engineering qualification.
\end{abstract}
