\begin{abstract}
Published engineering knowledge is commonly distributed across equations, tables, figures, narrative assumptions, and solver-specific results that are difficult to reuse computationally without losing provenance or overstating validation. This paper presents \engiproof, a source-bounded engineering-evidence framework designed to convert selected published methods into reproducible calculations, independent checks, explicit comparisons, discrepancy records, evidence graphs, and callable engineering methods. The framework separates extraction candidates from engineering evidence, distinguishes \eclass{PUBLISHED}, \eclass{INDEPENDENT}, and \eclass{SOLVER\_NEW} evidence, and keeps computational verification separate from engineering qualification.

The current development adds a conservative paper-ingestion pipeline comprising source fingerprinting, source-identity review, target discovery, missing-equation recovery, structure extraction, selected-target readiness gates, reproduction planning, discrepancy classification, human discrepancy decisions, and evidence-graph synchronization. Two pipe-in-pipe case studies are used as development stress tests. They demonstrate successful reproduction of selected published mechanics while also exposing source-backed numerical and unit inconsistencies that are retained rather than removed through parameter tuning. These examples motivate the central argument of the paper: for engineering reuse, paper-to-code conversion is insufficient; a useful system must explicitly track what was published, what was independently reconstructed, what agrees, what does not, and what remains unqualified.

This draft reports bounded case evidence only. Broader claims about generality and extraction performance are reserved for a later evaluation across additional heterogeneous papers.
\end{abstract}
