\section{Source ingestion and structural extraction}
\label{sec:ingestion}

\subsection{Fingerprint and identity}
Each source is bound to a SHA-256 of its bytes. The PDF stays outside the repository, and a source can be re-opened for enrichment only if its fingerprint matches (PA-01).

A separate identity audit compares the configured title, year and DOI with what is recoverable from the source. It distinguishes a DOI \emph{absent} from the extractable text from a \emph{conflicting} one. The audit exists because of failures:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item The first real case (P40) was accepted at intake with a wrong DOI and year. A human found the error, and the gate was introduced in response (F1).
  \item A received/copyright year in P42 raised a false conflict, which was removed by distinguishing publication from secondary years (F6).
  \item The 1983 scan (P45) contains no DOI at all, so the audit reports \texttt{NOT\_FOUND\_IN\_SOURCE} and identity was confirmed manually.
  \item For P43 and P44 the DOIs were left pending at ingestion. The P43 SPE paper number was recorded wrongly and corrected later from publisher records (F18).
\end{itemize}
The identity audit is therefore a gate with a human in the loop. It is not an automatic detector (PA-02).

\subsection{Candidates, structures and readiness}
Figures, tables, equations and appendices are first recorded as candidates with locators. Structure extraction then attempts caption-anchored table rows and equation bodies. For a selected target, a \emph{readiness} gate opens only when:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item a table has a true caption with candidate data rows; or
  \item an equation has a recovered body.
\end{itemize}
Readiness permits reproduction work to start. It does not make extracted text evidence, and the promotion gate independently requires reviewed targets (PA-03).

Extraction was hardened against concrete, recorded failures rather than against a benchmark:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item Type1/CFF glyph placeholders in equation text and lost grouped references such as ``Eqs.~(2), (3), (6), and (9)'' (P40, ASCE; F2, F3);
  \item punctuation-free ASME captions that were not treated as anchors (P41; F4);
  \item Elsevier split captions (P42; F7);
  \item a 1983 bilevel scan with an OCR layer, on which automated readiness reached only 4 of 16 selected targets and missed an equation, sub-figures and appendices (P45; F16).
\end{itemize}
In the P45 case, every figure and appendix was then reviewed manually from page images, and the review is recorded in the study manifest.

\subsection{Tracked, text-free ingestion summaries}
Intake directories can hold source excerpts, so they are local and excluded from version control by design. To make ingestion outcomes auditable without redistributing text, each study carries a tracked \texttt{ingestion\_summary.json}. It records only hashes, counts, statuses and target identifiers:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item the ingestion route;
  \item the fingerprint, and whether it matches the study manifest;
  \item the identity-audit checks;
  \item candidate and structure counts by kind and status;
  \item selected-target readiness;
  \item the source format;
  \item whether manual review was required.
\end{itemize}
The source format (born-digital, scan with a text layer, or image-only) is detected from page-sized raster images, and only when the local PDF matches the fingerprint. For P45 it detects a scan with a text layer at 300~dpi on all 11 pages.

Where an intake is not available, the summary says so explicitly and lists only outcomes already written in tracked project records, with references. It never estimates. Four cases (P40--P43) still await a machine-generated summary from the owner's local intakes, and P44 has no recorded pipeline ingestion. This is reported as it stands (PA-04).

No extraction accuracy or recall is claimed (PA-05). The case-level observations above are failure-driven hardening, not a performance measure.
