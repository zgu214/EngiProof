\section{Introduction: the problem is silent normalisation}
\label{sec:intro}

Engineering papers often contain enough information to reproduce part of a method, but not a complete model. Reusing them computationally therefore requires more than extracting equations or generating code. The difficulty is sharpest when a source is internally inconsistent or incomplete, which is common. Three examples from the cases studied here illustrate it.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item A table prints an eigenvalue of 13.221. The same paper's approximation formula, its own error table and an independent finite-element solution all give 18.221 (\S\ref{sec:discrepancy}, P43-D001).
  \item A table reports a friction increment of 471~N/m split into 153 and 312~N/m, which sum to 465~N/m. The paper's own load-sharing equations give 158.8 and 312.2~N/m (P41-D001).
  \item A prescribed riser head tension is printed as ``298'' with no unit (P45-D003).
\end{itemize}

A conventional paper-to-code workflow resolves each of these silently. It substitutes the ``obvious'' correction, chooses a unit, or adjusts an input until the printed number appears. The resulting program may be numerically sound and still untraceable. A later reader cannot tell which values were published, which were inferred, and which were forced.

\engiproof{} is built on the opposite rule: \emph{disagreement is evidence and is preserved}. It aims to convert a published method into computational evidence whose status is explicit at every step.
\begin{center}
\textbf{published source} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{source-bounded reproduction} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{independent check} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{comparison / discrepancy} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{human decision} $\rightarrow$ \textbf{callable method},
\end{center}
with engineering qualification kept outside the chain.

\paragraph{Contributions.} The paper makes the following contributions, each bounded by the evidence in Appendix~\ref{app:claims}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em]
  \item An evidence model that separates extraction candidates, evidence classes (\eclass{PUBLISHED}, \eclass{INDEPENDENT}, reserved \eclass{SOLVER\_NEW}), per-target outcomes and engineering qualification (\S\ref{sec:framework}).
  \item Source-bounded reproduction and methodologically independent checking that decide the direction of a conflict rather than being tuned to it (\S\S\ref{sec:reproduction}--\ref{sec:independent}).
  \item Discrepancy preservation with a controlled taxonomy and an append-only human decision boundary that gates promotion (\S\ref{sec:discrepancy}).
  \item Non-mutating verification. The framework's own verification was found to rewrite frozen evidence; the defect was inventoried, and the fix distinguishes byte, numerical and engineering equivalence across operating systems (\S\ref{sec:nonmutation}).
  \item A capability-organised evaluation on six heterogeneous studies, including a register of the framework's own failures (\S\S\ref{sec:evaluation}--\ref{sec:failures}).
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What is not claimed.} This paper does not claim the following:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
  \item extraction accuracy or recall, since no labelled benchmark exists;
  \item completeness of inconsistency detection;
  \item organisational independence of the checks;
  \item generality beyond offshore and subsea structural mechanics;
  \item autonomous scientific discovery;
  \item any form of engineering qualification.
\end{itemize}
Software agents were used to develop the framework. That is a method detail, not a result.
